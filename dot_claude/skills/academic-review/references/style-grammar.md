# Style and Grammar Guide

Rules for clear scientific communication.

## Voice and Attribution

### Use Active Voice
Active voice clearly distinguishes your work from cited work. This is an ethical issue of credit attribution.

**Passive (unclear who did what):**
> The sample was prepared and measurements were taken.

**Active (clear attribution):**
> We prepared the sample and measured the conductivity.

### Specify Actors
When citing, make clear what others did vs. what you did:

**Good:**
> Smith et al. observed X in system A. Here we extend this approach to system B.

**Problematic:**
> X was observed in system A. The approach was extended to system B.

## Clarity

### Avoid Pronouns
Replace vague pronouns with specific nouns:

| Unclear | Clear |
|---------|-------|
| "that" | "that voltage signal" |
| "this" | "this temperature dependence" |
| "it" | "the peak" |
| "the modulation" | "the z modulation" |

### Use Adjectives
When any doubt exists, add clarifying adjectives:

| Ambiguous | Clear |
|-----------|-------|
| "the signal" | "the optical signal" |
| "the transition" | "the CDW transition" |
| "the mode" | "the 2 THz mode" |

### Parallel Structure
Items in lists or "and" statements should be the same part of speech:

**Wrong:**
> We demonstrated superconductivity and the observation of phonons.

**Right:**
> We demonstrated superconductivity and observed phonons.

**Right:**
> We observed superconductivity and phonon modes.

## Acronyms

### Minimize Usage
- Keep total acronyms to 5-10 maximum
- If unfamiliar to reader, adds mental clutter
- Often better to write it out

### Don't Capitalize Before Defining
**Wrong:** Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM)
**Right:** scanning tunneling microscopy (STM)

**Wrong:** liquid Mercury (Hg)
**Right:** liquid mercury (Hg)

### Define at First Use
After definition, use acronym consistently. Don't switch back and forth.

## Word Choice

### Words to Avoid or Use Carefully

| Word | Rule |
|------|------|
| "significantly" | Only for statistical significance |
| "successfully" | Delete - let results speak |
| "remarkably" | Delete - amplifier |
| "very" | Usually delete |
| "unique" | Rarely true - be specific |
| "obviously" | Dismissive - delete |
| "hopefully" | Vague - state what you expect |

### Commonly Confused Words

**only** - Place immediately before word it modifies:
- "I only bought groceries" = I didn't dance at the store
- "I bought only groceries" = I didn't buy party balloons

**its / it's**
- "its" = possessive (the sample and its properties)
- "it's" = contraction of "it is"

**fewer / less**
- "fewer" = discrete items (fewer samples)
- "less" = continuous quantities (less current)

**which / that**
- "which" = non-restrictive clause (can be removed)
- "that" = restrictive clause (essential for meaning)

**affect / effect**
- "affect" = verb (X affects Y)
- "effect" = noun (the effect of X on Y)
- Exception: "effect" as verb means "to bring about"

## Numbers and Units

### Always Include Units
**Wrong:** The temperature was 200.
**Right:** The temperature was 200 K.

### Significant Figures
- Use reasonable precision
- Report errors where appropriate
- Explain error determination method
- Be consistent throughout document

### Equations
- Number all equations
- Define all symbols (before or immediately after)
- Check dimensional correctness
- Use `\noindent` after mid-paragraph equations

## Formatting

### Spacing
Use backslash-space after mid-sentence periods:
- `e.g.\ correct` → e.g. correct
- `Fig.\ 2` → Fig. 2

### Math and Symbols
| Incorrect | Correct |
|-----------|---------|
| cosθ | cos θ |
| Tsample | T_sample |
| Vrms | V_rms |
| B=5T | B = 5 T |
| 1st | 1st (with superscript) |

### Hyphenation
Hyphenate adjectival compounds:
- "well-known effect" (before noun)
- "the effect is well known" (after noun, no hyphen)

## Redundancy

### Text vs. Captions
Don't repeat information. When in doubt:
- Detailed information belongs in caption
- Main text refers to figure for details

### Results vs. Discussion
- Results: what the data shows
- Discussion: what it means and how it adds to knowledge
- Don't repeat results in discussion
